Daybezel — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 November 2026

Daybezel is an alarm clock for iPhone made by Oleksandr Vovchuk (“we”, which is one person). This policy says what the app does with your information. The short version: everything stays on your phone, and we never see it.

What the app stores, and where

  • Your alarms: the time, the name you gave each one (for example “Wake up”), how many times it rings, whether it repeats, and the sound and snooze settings. They are saved in a file inside the app’s private storage on your iPhone. A copy is kept in the app’s own shared folder on the same phone so its widgets and its Lock Screen card can show your next alarm.
  • Your alarm names: the names you used before, so the app can offer them again when you set a new alarm. You can remove one with a long press.
  • Your settings: the defaults for new alarms (sound, rings, snooze), haptics and the dial’s orientation. They are saved in the app’s preferences on your iPhone.
  • A record of rings: when an alarm was scheduled, rang, was stopped or snoozed, and any scheduling error. It is a small file on your iPhone that the app uses itself, for example so it never asks you for a rating right around an alarm. The app has no button to view or send it.

Nothing on this list leaves your phone. iCloud backup of your phone may include the app’s files like any other app’s; that is Apple’s backup, under your Apple account and your control.

What we collect

Nothing. Daybezel has no account, no sign-in, no server and no network connection. It contains no analytics, no advertising, no crash-reporting service and no third-party software development kits. We cannot see your alarms, your names for them, or when you use the app. The App Store listing says “Data Not Collected”, and that is the whole truth.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Alarms (AlarmKit). Daybezel asks iOS for permission to schedule alarms, so they can ring even when the app is closed or the phone is in Silent mode. The first time you add an alarm, the app explains why on a short card, and then iOS asks. You can change your answer at any time in iPhone Settings → Daybezel. Without it, the app can draw the dial but cannot ring.

The app does not ask for your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone, health data or calendar.

Sharing

The app does not share data with anyone, because it has none to share. If you write to us from the Support row in Settings, the email is written and sent by you from your own Mail app, with only the app’s version filled in; we use it only to help you and delete it when the problem is solved.

Deleting your data

Delete an alarm in the app and it is gone from the file. Delete the app and iOS removes everything it stored, including the record of rings and the widgets’ copy. There is nothing on our side to delete.

Children

Daybezel is rated 4+ and contains nothing unsuitable for children, but it is not designed for them and does not knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If a future version ever adds something that collects or sends data (for example an optional sync), this page will say so before that version is released, and the App Store privacy label will be updated with it.
