Daybezel — Support

Daybezel is a free alarm clock for iPhone that shows the next 24 hours as one dial. It is made by one person, Alex Vovchuk, in Ukraine.

Contact

Email wishu.developer@gmail.com and say which iPhone and iOS version you have. You will get an answer from Alex himself, usually within two days.

Common questions

An alarm did not ring. Daybezel schedules alarms with Apple’s AlarmKit, the system the built-in Clock uses, so they ring with the app closed, the phone locked, in Silent mode and through a Focus. Two things can stop that: Daybezel’s alarm permission is off (iPhone Settings → Daybezel → Alarms, turn it on; the dial shows a blocked state and a button when this is the case), or the phone was powered off at the alarm time (a phone that is off cannot ring; this is true for every alarm app). If neither applies, please email with the time of the alarm and what the dial showed afterwards; the app keeps a plain activity log you can share from Settings.

How do I move an alarm? Drag its pebble along the ring; the bar and the countdown follow. Each step is five minutes. For exact minutes, tap the pebble, then tap the time at the top of the sheet.

How do I delete an alarm? Swipe its row in the list, or tap the pebble and use the red “Delete alarm” row, or drag the pebble into the centre of the dial. Every delete can be undone from the message that appears.

What are rings? One alarm can ring several times a few minutes apart, for example 07:00, 07:05, 07:10 and 07:15. It is still one alarm on the dial, with small beads after it. Stop ends all of them; Snooze keeps the rest.

Why do I not see tomorrow’s alarms? The dial always shows the next 24 hours from now. An alarm further away appears when it comes within that window. Repeating alarms keep their schedule in the background.

Does Daybezel send my data anywhere? No. There is no account, no server and no analytics. Your alarms are stored on your phone. See the Privacy Policy.

Which iPhones and iOS versions? iPhone with iOS 26.1 or later. AlarmKit does not exist before iOS 26, and iOS 26.0 has known problems with alarm sounds, so 26.1 is the minimum. iPad is not supported yet.

Can I use my own sound? Version 1 ships with Daybezel’s own set of alarm sounds (soft risers, firm tones, chimes and a classic bell). Importing your own files is on the list for a later version.

Reporting a problem

Email the steps you took, what you expected and what happened, plus a screenshot if there is one. For a missed alarm, the activity log from Settings → Support → Share log helps most.
